Employment Act Quick Reference

Coverage & RM4,000 threshold

Employment Act protection generally extends to private-sector employees, but employees whose wages exceed RM4,000/month are excluded from statutory OT, rest-day/public-holiday premium pay and termination-benefit provisions unless they fall within specified First Schedule categories such as manual labour.

First Schedule; JTKSM Employment Act FAQ

Notice of termination

Written contractual notice governs where provided. If there is no written provision, the statutory minimum is 4 weeks (<2 years), 6 weeks (2–<5 years), or 8 weeks (≥5 years). The day notice is given is included.

Section 12

Pay in lieu of notice

Either party may terminate without serving all or part of the notice by paying indemnity equal to the wages that would have accrued during the notice or unexpired notice period.

Section 13(1)

Incomplete month wages

For monthly-rated employees who join after the first day, leave before month-end, or take unpaid leave, section 18A uses monthly wages ÷ days in the wage period × eligible days.

Section 18A; JTKSM wage guidance

Work on rest day

For monthly/weekly-rated covered employees: up to half normal hours = ½ ORP; more than half up to normal hours = 1 ORP; hours beyond normal hours = at least 2× HRP for the excess.

Section 60(3)

Normal-day overtime

Covered employees performing work beyond normal daily hours are entitled to at least 1.5× hourly rate of pay. The Employment (Limitation of Overtime Work) Regulations 1980 prescribe a 104-hour limit in any one month, subject to statutory permission mechanisms.

Section 60A(3)–(4); 1980 Overtime Limitation Regulations

Paid public holidays

Employees receive 11 gazetted paid holidays each calendar year. A covered employee required to work on a paid holiday receives the statutory holiday pay plus an additional 2 days' ORP; overtime beyond normal hours is at least 3× HRP.

Section 60D

Annual leave

Statutory annual leave is 8 / 12 / 16 days for service <2 / 2–<5 / ≥5 years. In a terminating service year, accrual is proportionate to completed months; fractions below ½ day are disregarded and ½ or more round to 1 day.

Section 60E

Part-time employee leave

Under the Employment (Part-Time Employees) Regulations 2010, statutory part-time annual leave is 6 / 8 / 11 days and paid sick leave is 10 / 13 / 15 days depending on service. Part-time employees also have separate public-holiday and extra-hours rules.

Employment (Part-Time Employees) Regulations 2010, regs. 7–8

Sick & hospitalisation leave

Non-hospital sick leave: 14 / 18 / 22 days depending on service. Hospitalisation leave is a separate 60-day entitlement, giving a total potential entitlement of 74 / 78 / 82 days.

Section 60F; JTKSM FAQ

Maternity

Maternity leave is at least 98 consecutive days. Normal commencement is not earlier than 30 days before confinement and not later than the day immediately following confinement. Maternity allowance has separate service/eligibility conditions.

Section 37

Paternity

A married male employee meeting the statutory conditions is entitled to 7 consecutive days paid paternity leave beginning on the confinement date, limited to five confinements.

Section 60FA; JTKSM FAQ

Working hours

Normal limits include 45 hours/week, generally 8 hours/day, no more than 5 consecutive hours without at least a 30-minute break, and a 10-hour spread-over. By agreement, up to 9 hours on some days may be used where other days are shorter.

Section 60A

Shift work

Shift workers may exceed 8 hours/day or 45 hours in an individual week, but the average over three weeks must not exceed 45 hours/week. Except specified emergencies, they should not be required to work more than 12 hours/day.

Section 60C

Termination / lay-off benefits

For eligible employees with at least 12 months' continuous service, minimum rates are 10 / 15 / 20 days' wages per year for service <2 / 2–<5 / ≥5 years, with incomplete years prorated by complete months.

Section 60J; Employment (Termination and Lay-Off Benefits) Regulations 1980

Retrenchment notification

Retrenchment, separation scheme, lay-off and pay-cut actions may trigger Borang PK notification obligations to JTKSM, including advance reporting timelines.

Section 63; JTKSM Retrenchment guidance

Wage payment deadlines

Regular wages are generally due no later than the 7th day after the wage period. Rest-day, public-holiday and OT payments are generally due by the last day of the next wage period. On normal termination, unpaid earned wages are due by the termination date; where the employee terminates without notice, earned wages are due by the third day after termination.

Sections 19–21

Flexible Working Arrangement response

An employer receiving a written Flexible Working Arrangement application must approve or refuse it in writing within 60 days of receipt; a refusal must state the reason.

Sections 60P–60Q; JTKSM FAQ
